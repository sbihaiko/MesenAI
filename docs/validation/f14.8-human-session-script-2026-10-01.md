`stack.xml` layer names, top to bottom, are

- four-layer files: `palettes`, `guides`, `paint`, `orig`;
- five-layer file: `palettes`, `guides`, `paint`, `context`, `orig`.

`paint` is visible and unlocked; `orig` is `edit-locked`; `guides` and
`palettes` are hidden and locked; `context` is visible at opacity 0.5.

**Two facts that shape what you can and cannot observe.**

1. **`stack.xml` cannot say which layer is selected.** OpenRaster has no
   `selected` attribute (ADR-0220 §3), so "`paint` selected" is not something a
   script can check in the file. It is a property of the program that opens it,
   and that is exactly what part A observes. The 2026-09-23 measurement
   (`docs/validation/f12.11-stop3-and-gimp-findings-2026-09-23.md` §8): GIMP 2.10
   and Krita 5.3.4 both open an `.ora` with the **bottom** layer, `orig`,
   active, whatever the stack order. The decision (option (b)) is therefore that
   stop (2) reads "`paint` selected **after the documented click**".
2. **A recorded figure sheet has four layers, not five.** The PRD's bounded
   input says "one Contra figure sheet (recorded, five layers)". By the
   ADR-0220 §3 amendment (2026-09-22) `context` exists only on a surface whose
   every cell has a stage position, which today is the `artist_map.py`
   panorama; figure, scenery and CHR sheets have four. So the five-layer file
   staged here is the Contra stage-1 panorama, and the recorded figure sheet is
   a four-layer file. If the person who owns the PRD wants a figure sheet with
   five layers, that needs a recorder change, not a session.

## 4. Part B — F12.5: the hand-added overflow cell (about 25 minutes)

2026-09-19 log used `pose001` (6 members); in the staged recording `pose001` is

Make a work copy and open the editor **on the copy** (an export writes into the

