# F14.4 — ADR-0230 palette gap measured (2026-09-24)

The measurement ADR-0230 ("What decides it") asks for before a human picks an
option: split the drawn keys no sheet cell reaches into brightness folds and
colourways, then prototype each option still standing and measure drawn-key
coverage, `sheets/` size and the `mep_build` round trip. ADR-0230 stays
**`proposed`**, Decision "Not decided". This log measures and records. It
decides nothing and ships nothing. Every prototype lived in a session
scratchpad and was thrown away. Nothing under `Core/` or `scripts/` changed.
Raw material (packs, logs, scripts) is not versioned. This log keeps the
numbers and the code needed to repeat them.

## Headline

1. **Colourways are a real share.** Castlevania has **61 of 96** missing
   keys that change a painted entry's hue against the palette the sheet cell
   carries (58 of them sprites). Zelda has **45 of 312** under the same
   pairwise test and **128 of 312** under `compute_folds`' own pack-wide
   grouping. The rest are folds: identical RGB (Castlevania 29, Zelda 133) or
   a step of a fade (Castlevania 6, Zelda 134).
2. **(c) and (d) both reach 100 % of drawn keys with 0 unobserved keys.**
   (c) was simulated at the Python level and (d) prototyped in a scratch
   copy of `mep_build.py`. (d) reaches them only once the cell is painted,
   and until then it rebuilds byte-identical to today. (c) reaches them with
   nothing painted. Both round-trip with 0 errors and a byte-identical
   second build.
3. **(b) does not do what the ADR expects, measured with the Core's own
   loader.** A pack loads `textures/` over `auto/` (`NesConsole`,
   `MergeLowerLayer`). There the auto layer's own exact `defaultTile=N`
   rule for every drawn palette wins over the wildcard. So (b) serves **0**
   of the 96 / 312 missing keys, and only takes over palettes the recording
   **never drew** (53/53 and 124/124 probes). With `textures/` loaded alone
   it serves all the missing keys, and every unobserved palette too. It
   does beat the bootstrap's neutral-palette `Y` rule in both setups.

## Step 1: folds vs colourways

A missing key `(shape, P)` is compared with every palette a sheet entry of
the same shape carries (Castlevania always 1, Zelda at most 2). The
predicates are `artist_chr_kit`'s own: `painted_indices`, `fade_related`,
`fold_measure` and `HUE_DRIFT_GATE_DEG` = 25°.

- **Pairwise to the cell** (what (b)/(d) would render from the painted cell):
  - *inert*: same RGB on every painted entry;
  - *fade*: `P` is the cell's palette further down the same ramps, drift
    ≤ 25°;
  - *brighter*: the cell's palette is a fade of `P`, so rebuilding `P` needs
    a Brightness above 1;
  - *colourway*: a painted entry changes hue.
- **`compute_folds` as written**: run over every drawn key, one fake page per
  key. The key folds when it lands in the same fold group as a palette the
  sheets carry. This is stricter: it takes the brightest palette as the base
  and refuses a step whose drift *against that base* exceeds the gate.

Reading it:

- **Castlevania is a sprite colourway problem.** 58 of its 61 colourways are
  sprites whose base cell is on a `sprite`/`sprites` sheet. The 12-key shape
  is the blank tile, all inert.
- **Zelda is mostly a fade problem.** 79 shapes miss exactly 3 palettes.
  That is the 4-step screen fade (`3617370F` → `3617270F` → `3617170F` →
  `3617070F` on the orange ramp), and the sheet cell carries one step
  (`3617270F`). The two criteria disagree on 83 keys, and 74 of them are
  here: a step folds onto the cell's palette within 25°, but `compute_folds`
  measures it against the brightest step and refuses it. Zelda's 45
  pairwise colourways sit mostly on sprite sheets (28). Another 7 are the
  blank tile.
- **ADR-0230's split test lands on its second branch.** Colourways are not
  negligible on either game. A fold-only (b) or (d) would leave 61 keys
  (Castlevania) and 45–128 keys (Zelda) where they are today.

## Steps 2–4: the options

What was built:

- **(a)** needs no prototype. It is today's numbers.
- **(b) prototype**: a scratch copy of `mep_build.py`, 18 lines added. An
  edited crop whose shape has a drawn palette (a `defaultTile=N` row of the
  key source) that no sidecar entry carries is emitted with
  `defaultTile=Y`.
- **(d) prototype**: a scratch copy of `mep_build.py`, 5 lines in
  `_cell_crops`. A sidecar tile entry may carry `"palettes": [...]`. An
  *edited* crop is also emitted under each listed palette, with that key's
  own attributes from the key source, so each is a recorded `N` rule. An
  untouched crop emits nothing extra. A scratch script wrote the missing
  palettes onto one entry per shape: the entry on the highest-ranked sheet,
  later sidecar name on ties, which is the one `mep_build`'s precedence picks.
The two build prototypes, as they were applied to scratch copies of
`scripts/mep_build.py` at `90703652`:

- **(c) simulation** (Python, not the recorder): one new cell per missing
  key, appended in new rows at the bottom of the sheet that owns the shape's
  base cell. It is not placed beside the base cell as ADR-0230 describes.
  The cell holds that one 8×8 tile, and its other sub-tiles are null, so no
  unobserved key is invented for the rest of a metatile. Its pixels are the
  recording's own nearest-neighbour art for that key (the `Chr_*.orig.png`
  crop, which is what 1 108/1 626 and 398/553 existing unmirrored sheet
  crops equal), with a 1× copy in the `*.orig.png` twin. `mep_build` itself
  was not modified for (c).
- **"Painted"** means one pixel changed in the owner cell of every affected
  shape (48 cells on Castlevania, 89 on Zelda). It is the same edit in every
  arm.
- **Round trip**: copy the recorded pack, copy `auto/textures` to
  `textures`, run `mep_build.py build <copy> --quiet` twice, then compare
  the two `textures/hires.txt`.
- **Render equality** compares, per `(condition, tile, palette)` rule, the
  sha1 of the crop's pixels plus the Brightness and `defaultTile` fields
  against today's rebuild in the same paint state.
- **Served** (for (b)): a scratch C++ harness linked against the same
  dylib. It loads with `HdPackLoader::LoadHdNesPack`, optionally merges
  `auto/` under it with `HdPackLoader::MergeLowerLayer` (the `NesConsole`
  path), and resolves each key as `HdNesPack::GetMatchingTile` does for a
  CHR RAM tile: the exact key, then `GetKey(true)`, then the first
  unconditioned entry. It measures load and merge order. It does not run
  the renderer.

## What each option costs, measured

- **(a)**: nothing. On Zelda, 54.4 % of drawn keys stay paintable only on
  the pattern pages. At run time a layered pack still renders them from
  `auto/` with the recorded art. They are not lost, only out of context.
- **(b)**: no sheet or sidecar change. It is defeated by the layering the
  Core already does: with `auto/` present it changes only unobserved
  palettes, which ADR-0183 §3 forbids. Without `auto/` it serves every
  palette of the shape. It also has two cost the ADR did not list:
  - A wildcard rule has one Brightness, so it can reproduce only the
    *inert* folds (29 / 133). A fade step renders at the painted cell's
    brightness.
  - The drawn-palette evidence is gone from the key source after the first
    build. The prototype kept `Y` only because `mep_build` carries row
    attributes over. An implementation would have to read the drawn set
    from `auto/`.
- **(c)**: +96 / +312 cells (+61 / +76 folded), +1.3 % / +12.2 % bytes.
  - Coverage is complete without painting, and each colourway is its own
    cell.
  - The sheet layout changes, as the ADR says. The simulation appended rows,
    so existing cells kept their coordinates and today's rules rendered
    identically. A beside-the-base layout would move cells.
  - The real change is in the recorder. `HdPackBuilder.cpp` is at 2 427 of
    its 2 428-line ADR-0137 ceiling.
- **(d)**: a schema field, 5 build lines in the prototype, and +0.07 % /
  +0.6 % bytes.
  - Coverage is complete once the owner cell is painted, and the default
    render is today's.
  - A painted colourway recolours its twins (Castlevania 61, Zelda 45–128
    keys) until the artist splits them off.
  - The prototype emits each listed key at its recorded Brightness (1). A
    fade step then renders at full brightness once painted. A real (d)
    would write the fold's Brightness for fades (Zelda 134 keys). The
    *brighter* case (Castlevania 6, Zelda 40) needs a Brightness above 1.
    The loader parses one (`HdPackLoader`, `int Brightness`), but the
    render was not checked.

## What the numbers point to (not a decision)

- (b) is ruled out as written: in the setup packs actually load in, it
  serves unobserved palettes and none of the missing ones.
- Colourways are a real share, so a fold-only variant of (b) or (d) is not
  enough on its own.
- (c) and (d) both reach 100 % of drawn keys with no invented key and a
  clean round trip. They trade differently:
  - (c) shows every colourway on the sheet and needs no painting to be
    complete. It costs +12 % sheet bytes on Zelda and a recorder change
    against a full line ceiling.
  - (d) is a small Python and schema change that keeps today's sheets
    byte-for-byte. It leaves colourways implicit until split.
- A combination the numbers make natural, untested: (d) for folds (with
  their Brightness) and (c) for colourways, only 61 / 45–128 extra cells.

## Observations outside ADR-0230's options

- **Sheet art is not the recording's art.** 901 of today's 912 rebuilt
  Castlevania rules (360 of 367 on Zelda) render different pixels from the
  same key in `auto/`. The sheets are nearest-neighbour 4× crops
  (`Chr_*.orig.png`). The auto layer's `Chr_*.png` pages are xBRZ-smoothed.
  An unpainted rebuild therefore looks crisper than the recording. It
  predates this ADR and was not filed.
- **#415 is already fixed on `main`** (`10176a10`, 2026-09-24), so
  ADR-0230's Context sentence about it describes the past. The first-seen
  rule still decides which palette a cell carries.

## Caveats

- The same two CHR RAM recordings as ADR-0229. A CHR ROM game was not
  measured.
- (c) is a Python simulation of the recorder's output. It does not show the
  cost inside `HdPackBuilder`, the cycle-grid and map readability ADR-0230
  lists, or a beside-the-base layout.
- The (b) serving measurement uses the Core's loader and merge but a copy of
  the lookup, and it counts the first *unconditioned* entry as served. In
  some lookups a conditioned rule comes first: 81 (Castlevania) and 43
  (Zelda) of the missing-key lookups, and 40 / 24 of the unobserved ones
  under (b). Those are condition variants of the same key, each followed by
  its bare twin (ADR-0189 §3). In the rebuilt layer, `mep_build` emits every
  variant from one crop, so a condition that holds would pick the same art
  there. For the auto layer that was not checked.
- Painting was simulated by one changed pixel per owner cell. Precedence
  among several painted cells of one key (#253) was avoided, not measured.
