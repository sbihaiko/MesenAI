# F12.2 — the artist-surface cold read, 28 ROMs, Opus as the evaluator

**Date:** 2026-09-19
**Binary:** `main` @ `6fe5226a` (working tree, uncommitted guide and
`mep_build.py` changes; see *What moved under the runs*)
**Evaluator:** 28 fresh Opus sessions, one per game, one per dispatch
**Dispatcher:** this session, with `scripts/f122_prepare_evaluator.py`
**Briefing:** `docs/validation/f12.2-sweep-evaluator-briefing.md`

This is the ADR-0214 protocol -- a fresh session with no context from the
builder, briefed on the goal and not the path, forbidden to open
`hires.txt` -- run 28 times instead of twice, with Opus in the evaluator
seat instead of Fable. The user's go-ahead, verbatim:
*"depois que tudo funcionar, repita o teste com todas as ROMs, mas usando o
Opus no lugar do fable"*.

The point of the sweep is not 28 passes. It is that one game is a panel and
28 games are a sample, and the difference between "this game's pack is odd"
and "the feature is broken" only shows up at 28.

## Binary and inputs

Each game got its own sandbox folder, `~/f12.2-opus-sandbox/<game>/`, plus
four shared folders:

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `index/<game>.md` | that game's inputs, absolute paths, one file |
| `guides/` | the three artist guides, copied at 18:53 |
| `games/<game>/` | the evaluator's work copy of the pack |
| `frames/<game>.png` / `.mss` | the paused frame, and the same frame as a save state |
| `tilemaps/<game>.png` | that frame's tilemap as the viewer draws it, `col,row` labelled |
| `tilemap-copy/<game>.txt` | what `Copy as MEP sheet cell` returns for every tile of that tilemap |
| `tilemap-menu-labels.txt` | the viewer's right-click menu as the viewer labels it |
| `tools/`, `bin/` | `mep_build.py` and its 7 import siblings, `mep_lint.py`, `headless_record` |

The sandbox is the whole world; the ROM and its pack folder are the one
exception, named in the index. Everything else -- the checkout, the ADRs,
the PRD, the panel script, this log -- is out of bounds and a logged stop.

The frame is minted per game rather than taken at power-on (ADR-0214 §2's
"start the clock when the paused frame is on screen"): the dispatcher
records each ROM with the bootstrap, then repaints every crop its built
`hires.txt` names in an index-encoded colour, in **both** the human layer
and the `auto/` layer, and picks the frame at which the second-most rules
are drawn. Without that the sweep would hand a third of the games a title
screen where nothing is ever drawn by a rule.

**Criterion 1: 28 of 28.** Every evaluator found `Copy as MEP sheet cell`
in the dumped menu labels without being told which line, between 5 and 55
seconds from pause, and nobody picked `Copy tile (HD pack format)` one line
above it. The label works. This is the one thing the sweep measured that
the mechanical replay could not.

**Criterion 4: 28 of 28 runs, one failure.** The failure matters more than
the number.
The Tennis run grepped the rebuilt `hires.txt` at 19:03:19 to find out
whether its pasted key had landed as a `<tile>`, printed nothing, changed no
decision, and reported itself as a fail. It did that because the copy, the
paste and the build give the artist **no receipt of any kind**, so the only
way to answer "did that work?" was the one file the path exists to make
unnecessary. The gate held; what pushed against it was a missing feature.

**Criterion 3: 13 of 28 on the path as dispatched.** Fifteen runs repainted
the right shape and could not see it. Every one of the fifteen proved the
repaint was correct before reporting the failure -- by moving
`textures/backgrounds/` aside, by bisecting `chr/`, by painting an existing
cell as a control, by re-rendering with the key's bank corrected. That is
the shape of a real defect and not a confused evaluator: the paste works,
the build works, the screen does not change.

## What the runs found

Ranked by how many of the 28 runs it cost.

**1. A captured screen overrides every `<tile>` on the frame the artist is
given -- and cannot be retired. 24 of 28 runs name it.** ADR-0050 and
ADR-0156 make the capture's precedence deliberate; what is not deliberate is
that the artist has no way out of it. Keeping `textures/backgrounds/` makes
the repaint a silent no-op. Deleting it makes `build` fail with one
`error: <background> ... does not exist` per file, because the build carries
`<background>` lines over from the previous `hires.txt` and never re-derives
them. The one action that works is the one the tool refuses. Filed as #339
(a capture winning on a frame it does not match, Punch-Out!!'s `STARRING` /
`LITTLE MAC` text, closest capture differing by 16 047 pixels) and #344 (it
cannot be retired). Tetris 2 walked every exit and found none: moving the
PNGs aside makes `build` re-emit the `<background>` lines from the key
source and lint fail; deleting the key source makes `build` refuse with
`no tile-key source`; `build --help` has no flag. It got its magenta by
deleting the captures from the *deployed* `mep/` by hand -- and the engine
drops the dangling entries harmlessly at load, so **the pack that works is
the one the toolchain calls invalid.** Tetris's run also showed the
captures can be *stale*:
with them in place the frame reads `LINES-000` / `SCORE 000000`, from the
live PPU state `LINES-001` / `SCORE 000085`.

**2. The copy emits a key the runtime never used, on CHR-banked games. 3
runs, and silent.** Dr. Mario: the copy returns `index 508` for a tile the
runtime drew as `252`; changing that one number and nothing else renders
30 720 magenta pixels. Lemmings: the copy returns `1` for a tile the pack
keys `0x1A01`, and `180` for one it keys `0x1AB4` -- `0x1A00 + 180 = 0x1AB4`
exactly. Ninja Gaiden: two shapes, two verbatim pastes, two pixel-identical
renders, while painting an *existing* cell produced 337 280 magenta pixels.
The key path resolves the CHR page from `_chrPages`, which holds the last
rendered scanline's mapping; the runtime resolves it per tile
(`Core/NES/HdPacks/HdBuilderPpu.h:59`). A wrong-bank key is well-formed, so
`build`, `lint` and the precedence line are all green. Filed as #341.

That cause was then reproduced in-process and narrowed further, and it is
**two** causes rather than one: Dr. Mario never splits mid-frame -- all 240
of its scanlines drew under one bank and the *paused* mapping is the
outlier, the game having rewritten the MMC1 bank before the vblank the
debugger sees -- while Lemmings and Ninja Gaiden really do switch CHR banks
mid-frame. `docs/adr/0215-a-copied-tile-key-is-resolved-through-the-mapping-that-drew-the-frame.md`
carries the four measurements, the rule that reproduces all three of #341's
numbers to the digit, and three questions a human had to pick; the user
picked them on 2026-09-19 and the ADR records which.

**3. The copy reads the live PPU palette.** Metroid: the copy hands out
`0F361506` for the title-screen logo and text; every rule the pack holds for
those bitmaps is keyed `0F0F0F0F`. Six verbatim pastes, six clean builds,
zero pixels changed. `mep_build` already has the message for this -- painting
all eight sheets magenta made it emit
`error: ... the repaint cannot affect that key at run time ... (#253)`
eleven times -- and it never fired for any of the six cells, which are
exactly that case. Filed as #342.

**4. The action named "Copy as MEP **sheet cell**" puts a `tiles[]` entry on
the clipboard.** The `index`/`x`/`y`/`count`/`context` around it, the
free-slot arithmetic and the sheet's scale are hand work -- four inferences
between the clipboard and a painted square, in a feature whose premise is
retiring that work. Every run stopped here. Filed as #340.

**5. A new cell whose key already exists is dropped without a word.** Dr.
Mario's first three pastes, Ninja Gaiden's second: build exit 0, no message,
no count, no effect. Indistinguishable from #341 by any signal the artist
has. Filed as #343.

**6. No receipt after the copy.** Every run that recorded criterion 8 said
the same thing: no toast, no status line, nothing that says a key was copied
or which tile it was. The first confirmation of any kind is a `build` line
two steps later, phrased in hex against a decimal `index`. This is what
pushed the Tennis run into `hires.txt` (above), and it is the reason four
runs burned minutes bisecting a pack to find out what the copy had done.
Filed as #340.

**7. `build`'s captured-screen warning answers a different question and never
names the edited sheet.** Pac-Man measured 19 sheets warned about and the
sheet the artist actually edited silent: `screen_shadowed_cells()`
(`scripts/mep_build.py:907`) keys on `c.get("metatile")` in
`adjacency.json`, so a freshly added cell is invisible to it. Filed as #338.

## The dispatcher's own defects

These are the dispatcher's, not the feature's, and they are recorded because
a panel that hands the evaluator a wrong rule is measuring itself.

**1. The briefing handed over a rule that does not exist.** Step 3 said the
cell's own `context` tells you which sheet a key came from. The copy
carries no `context` at all, and every cell of *both* `misc.json` and
`unsorted.json` reads `"context": "misc"` in the packs that ship both.
18 of 28 runs spent a stop on it. It was rewritten in the source briefing at
18:59:37 -- **after** the sandbox copy was made at 18:53:28, so all 28 runs,
including the 8 dispatched later, read the same stale text. One briefing for
the whole sweep, which is at least a clean fact to report. The guide was
fixed separately and now carries *Which sheet a copied key goes on*.

**2. The `context` claim was mine, not the feature's.** It came from reading
the sidecar field and assuming it routed the copy. Eleven runs of evidence
were needed to show the copy shares no field with the cell it is pasted
into. The same false sentence had been in the Fable briefing the day before;
that run never depended on it, so it survived.

**3. The tilemap and the save state were captured at different moments on
some games.** Bubble Bobble's run proved it: the tilemap shows the full
"Bubble Bobble" logo, the save state renders none of it, and a 195-key probe
found exactly 2 of the tilemap's keys on the rendered frame, both blank
tiles. From that run alone, "no background shape on this frame is
recolourable" and "the frame cannot pass" are the same observation. Tetris
named the same thing from the other side: the sandbox's two files are both
right and describe different layers.

**4. `tools/` shipped incomplete.** The first sandbox carried
`mep_build.py` and `mep_lint.py` alone; `mep_build.py` imports six siblings
it did not have. Found by the SMB3 validation run, fixed by computing the
AST import closure (8 modules) and copying all of them, verified by building
from the sandbox alone.

**5. The tilemap PNG is illegible at the scale it was handed over.** Four
runs said so and fell back to the copy table for coordinates. The image is
correct and the labels cannot be read at that size.

**6. `headless_record`'s `mesen-home` lands beside the prefix folder, not
under it.** A shallow prefix silently mixes games' identically-named
screenshots; the SMB run found the collision in its own output directory.

**7. Nothing states the pack's `scale`.** Six runs derived it from the
`.png` / `.orig.png` size ratio and said so; two said a wrong factor would
paint the wrong cell and still exit 0.

**8. The tilemap dump is one nametable and the visible window is not
marked.** Zelda II's run picked row 0 -- a full-width rock band scrolled off
the top -- built it, linted it, deployed it and rendered, all exit 0, and
got a byte-identical frame; the only way to tell was reversing the scroll
offset by eye. Contra's run had to derive a +22-tile scroll from the `PLAY`
text. On Zelda II none of the frame's water, bushes or rock ledges have a
line in the copy table at all, which makes the briefing's "pick a bush,
rock, ground or wall tile" unsatisfiable on that game.

**9. Some packs have no free-form sheet.** Zelda II ships neither
`misc.json` nor `unsorted.json`; every cell's `context` is `"scene"`, so
even the corrected sheet rule has nothing to say. The run reverse-engineered
`metatiles.json`'s grid (18x17, 8 free slots at y=273) to find a slot.

## What is not evaluated

- **Pointer discoverability.** Criterion 1 is scored on a label dump. This
  machine cannot open an Avalonia context-menu flyout: the AX tree does not
  expose it and `System Events` keystrokes do not reach the window. Nothing
  here says a right-click opens the flyout, that hover is not mistaken for a
  click, or that the entry is reachable without a keyboard.
- **Two ROMs, proven not evaluable rather than failed.** Double Dragon and
  Mega Man recorded their whole attract run as `<background>`: painting every
  crop the built `hires.txt` names turns 983 040 of 983 040 pixels magenta,
  which is the whole frame. There is no rule-drawn pixel at any frame those
  two recordings produce, so no sheet edit can reach the screen. They are
  excluded, with the measurement, not scored.
- **Would a person return.** Criterion 8 is an observation. A model that has
  never shipped a pack from a spreadsheet saying it would switch is not a
  revealed preference, and this sweep does not claim otherwise.
- **Criterion 5's pixel oracle at 28 games.** The mechanical scorer
  (`scripts/f122_score_panel.py`) reads the painted crop, the round-trip
  `<tile>` and `mep_lint.py` for each pack that has a log. Its table is
  below; where it and an evaluator's own report disagree, the scorer is
  right about criteria 5 and 6 and the evaluator is right about 1, 4, 7
  and 8.

  It took three attempts to make criterion 5 mean anything, and the two
  wrong ones are worth stating because each produced a confident false
  FAIL on a pack that works.

  1. Check that *a* `<tile>` exists at the painted crop. Nearly free: a crop
     can carry a rule from a different key and pass.
  2. Check the key against field 0 of the `<tile>` line. Field 0 is the
     image index; the key is field 1. False FAIL on Excitebike.
  3. Field 1, compared as text. `mep_build` writes the index in hex and not
     at a fixed width -- Excitebike's rule for 387 reads `0183`,
     Bubble Bobble's for 38 reads `26` -- so a padded comparison false-FAILs
     Bubble Bobble, whose evaluator's own cell declares 38 and whose rule
     carries `26`.

  The sweep could catch all three because 13 games had already been observed
  to pass by hand. A criterion-5 number produced without those controls
  would have read as the run's biggest finding twice over. The final check
  is the one that means something, and the scorer now agrees with the
  evaluators where both can speak: Bubble Bobble's 32 768 magenta pixels are
  the same block its log names by eye, cols 12-19 rows 2-6.

## What moved under the runs

The working tree changed while the sweep ran, and a reader of the logs will
see it:

- `docs/remastering-a-game.md` gained *What a cell is* and *Which sheet a
  copied key goes on*, and lost a step 1 that told the artist to
  `rm -rf <rom>/<stem>/mep`. The sandbox `guides/` were refreshed at 18:53
  and then again after the fix; runs before and after may quote different
  text. Several logs cite the new sections, and Donkey Kong's cites
  *What a cell is* while noting that both earlier cold reads had to
  reverse-engineer it.
- `scripts/mep_build.py` and `scripts/test_mep_build.py` gained message
  changes (#337). The sandbox `tools/` copy is the version the runs used.
- The briefing fix at 18:59:37 reached the source and not the sandbox
  (dispatcher defect 1).

## Honest limits

- One evaluator model, one dispatch each, 20 minutes each. A run that ran
  out of budget is scored by what it reached, not by what it would have.
- The frames are the dispatcher's choice, and they are the single largest
  influence on criterion 3: 24 of 28 runs name a capture on the frame the
  dispatcher picked. A sweep that picked frames by "the most rules are
  drawn here" would report a higher criterion 3 and would be measuring a
  friendlier question than the one an artist asks.
- Two dispatcher defects (1 and 3) mean the sheet-routing stop and the
  frame/tilemap mismatch counts are floors, not measures.
- Every number above is from a log in `out/log-<game>.md` under the sandbox,
  not from a summary. Where a claim here is a mechanism rather than a
  measurement, it is named as one: #341's cause is the two address paths
  disagreeing, and which of the two is wrong was left to the fix.
