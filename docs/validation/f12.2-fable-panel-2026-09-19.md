## Both runs found the same two dispatcher defects, and that is the finding

Each session was dispatched twice. **Round 1 stopped both times on the same two
bugs in the *dispatcher*, fixed before round 2:**

1. `tilemap-copy-<game>.txt` was keyed in **pixels** (`96,64`) while
   `tilemap-<game>.png` is labelled in **tiles** (`12,8`), and the briefing said
   the two matched. A reader who counts a square off a picture could not look
   its line up. The scan now emits `col,row` in tile units — the content is
   identical, which is how the defect was confirmed as a keying bug and not a
   reading bug.
2. The briefing's screenshot path was `out/<game>-mesen-home/Screenshots/`;
   `headless_record` puts `mesen-home` beside the output prefix's **parent**, so
   the real path is `out/<game>/mesen-home/Screenshots/`. Both runs went looking
   in the wrong place with the render already on disk.

Neither was reachable by the mechanical replay, by the headless test, or by the
session that wrote the protocol: every one of them already knows where the file
is and what the coordinate means. This is the concrete argument for ADR-0214
that a green suite is not a panel — and it is why round 1's logs are the
evidence, not a footnote.

## Findings from the runs

Not fixed here except where noted. F12.2's code is on `main`; this task does
not touch it.

**1. The guides do not document the sheet-cell sidecar schema.** Both sessions,
in both rounds, had to reverse-engineer the `cells[]` fields
(`index`/`x`/`y`/`count`/`context`/`label`/`tiles`) and the fact that a 16×16
cell grid accepts a single-8×8-tile cell, by copying the shape of a
neighbouring cell. Confirmed against the repo: `docs/remastering-a-game.md` has
one `cells[]` example at line ~550 carrying `index` and `condition` only, and
the copy action hands over a `tiles[]` **entry**, never a whole cell. This is
the same gap the guide review on
`docs/remastering-a-game.md` is addressing; the doc fix belongs there, and
`mep_lint.py` accepting a single-tile cell is the part that needs a decision,
not a paragraph.

**2. The copy is silent.** Both runs logged it independently as the thing that
made them unsure the click had worked, and both put it in criterion 8's own
words. It matches the briefing's own statement ("There is no toast anywhere,
before or after the copy"), so it is recorded as an observation, not a defect.
It is the strongest candidate in this run for a follow-up slice: the panel asks
a first-timer to trust a clipboard write they cannot see.

**3. `mep_build.py build`'s `<background>` warning names no scene.** Zelda's
build warns that captured screens draw over every `<tile>` rule, which made the
evaluator expect the magenta to be masked on the very scene where it then
worked. The warning is true and load-bearing (it is how
`docs/validation/f12.2-mechanical-replay-2026-09-19.md` finding 1 was found);
it just does not say which scenes. Wording, not behaviour.

**4. Sandbox hygiene.** A `rehearsal-2026-09-19/` folder from the round-1
dispatches sat in the sandbox unlisted by the briefing; the Zelda session
noticed it and correctly refused to open it. Moved to
`~/f12.2-rehearsal-2026-09-19/` — a cold read has to stay cold.

## Honest limits

- **Two games, one frame each.** Zelda's first overworld screen and Contra's
  stage-1 opening. A findable menu entry on two NES CHR sources says nothing
  about the GB/SMS/SNES viewers, about a CHR ROM game's third `index` field, or
  about a sprite viewer.
- **The reference frames are the ones the replay already asserts on**, picked
  because the recording did not capture them as a whole-screen
  `<background>`. That is a dispatcher choice, and it is a choice: the panel's
  own P4 ("play to a screen showing a shape you want to repaint") walks a
  human straight into the screens that *are* captured, where painting a cell
  renders nothing. The evaluators were kept out of that trap, so this run does
  not measure what happens to someone who is not.
- **The evaluators did not click a menu.** They read a label dump and a
  per-tile copy table the dispatcher produced with a headless test
  (`UI.HeadlessTests/CopyAsMepSheetCellTests.cs`, `MESEN_F122_*` gates).
  Criterion 1 measures whether the *label* is findable and unambiguous, not
  whether a real flyout opens, positions or responds. Pointer- and
  AX-level discoverability stays not evaluated.
- **Criterion 8 is an observation and stays one.** Two sentences, both quoted
  above, neither scored.
- **P15 is not a gate.** The elapsed figures are clock granularity of one
  minute, self-reported, and stop when the evaluator says the shape is on
  screen.
- **The evaluator is not an artist.** A capable model reading three guides is a
  proxy for a person's first twenty minutes; it is not a person. It had a
  perfect record on the two things it could not look up — the schema, and the
  silence — which is where a human would have burned time rather than noted a
  defect.
